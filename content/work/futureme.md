**Software Engineer** · Jan 2022 – Jan 2024 (2 years) · Remote, Brazil

I joined FutureMe as an intern when the platform was still in its early days — just a handful of basic activities live, everything else still to be built. Two years later I left as the engineer who had rewritten its core architecture, shipped its flagship product feature, and mentored the next wave of interns coming up behind me.

![FutureMe](/previews/futureme-2.png)

**What FutureMe does:** FutureMe is a Brazilian edtech startup that helps students figure out what to do with their lives. It's a gamified, interactive platform — closer in feel to Duolingo than a traditional careers-guidance tool — that walks students through activities and choices to help them discover a career path that fits them. It reached real adoption: 5,000+ students, across both public and private schools throughout Brazil.

**My role and growth:** I came in as an intern and adapted fast to the stack (Next.js, Pages Router), shipping frontend and backend features while redesigning the entire app UI around a "journey" metaphor — turning static screens into a guided path the student moves through. That work got me promoted from intern to full-time engineer within the year.

When the senior engineer on the team stepped away the following year, I moved into that role. The team grew, and I mentored two interns while still building hands-on.

**Core feature I built:** The heart of the product was the gamified journey dashboard itself — the interactive experience students actually lived in day to day. Rather than a static questionnaire, students moved through a structured journey: completing activities and challenges, unlocking achievements, and watching visual progress trackers fill in as they got closer to identifying a career path that fit them. I designed and built the UI and underlying logic for that entire experience, turning what could have been a linear form into something that felt like a game.

That same achievement and progress data fed the platform's capstone feature, which I also built: a printed, physical book generated for each student at the end of their journey. Every graph, choice, and achievement a student racked up on the platform was compiled and laid out dynamically, unique to that student, and rendered print-ready using React PDF — then sold as a purchasable keepsake. It meant taking the arbitrary, per-user data behind the gamified dashboard and turning it into a polished, professionally formatted physical artifact — not a UI concern most engineers ever have to solve, since it had to look right on paper, not just on a screen.
